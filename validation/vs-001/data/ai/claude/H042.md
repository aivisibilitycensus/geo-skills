# We tackle three questions accountants may have around MTD for VAT

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT has transformed how businesses and accountants handle VAT reporting in the UK. While many practices have successfully transitioned to digital record-keeping and submissions, questions still arise about best practices, compliance requirements, and how to maximize efficiency.

We've gathered three of the most common questions we hear from accountants about MTD for VAT and provided clear answers to help you serve your clients with confidence.

## 1. How can I efficiently manage MTD compliance for multiple clients?

One of the biggest challenges accountants face is managing MTD requirements across a diverse client base. Each business has unique needs, different transaction volumes, and varying levels of digital literacy.

The key to efficient multi-client management lies in standardizing your processes while using cloud accounting software that's designed for practice workflows. With Xero, you can manage all your clients from a single dashboard, accessing their financial data in real-time without switching between different systems or spreadsheets.

**Best practices for managing multiple clients include:**

- **Establishing consistent workflows:** Create standardized procedures for reviewing transactions, reconciling accounts, and submitting VAT returns that can be applied across your client base.

- **Setting up regular touchpoints:** Schedule quarterly or monthly check-ins with clients to review their VAT position, rather than scrambling at submission deadlines.

- **Leveraging practice management tools:** Use Xero Practice Manager or similar tools to track deadlines, assign tasks to team members, and ensure nothing falls through the cracks.

- **Automating where possible:** Take advantage of bank feeds, automated reconciliation features, and digital receipt capture to minimize manual data entry and reduce errors.

By creating repeatable systems and using integrated software, you can scale your practice without proportionally increasing your workload.

## 2. What should I do when clients aren't keeping digital records properly?

This is perhaps one of the most frustrating situations accountants encounter. MTD for VAT requires businesses to keep digital records and maintain a digital audit trail, but not all clients understand or prioritize this requirement.

When clients aren't maintaining proper digital records, you face several risks: compliance issues, inaccurate VAT submissions, and potential penalties that could damage your professional reputation.

**Here's how to address this challenge:**

**Education first:** Many clients simply don't understand what "digital records" means or why it matters. Take time to explain that MTD requires more than just submitting returns online—their underlying records must be digital too.

**Provide clear guidance:** Create simple documentation or checklists showing clients exactly what they need to do. Screenshots and step-by-step instructions can be invaluable for less tech-savvy clients.

**Offer training:** Consider hosting client workshops or one-on-one training sessions on basic bookkeeping in Xero. Even a 30-minute session can dramatically improve compliance.

**Build it into your service offering:** For clients who struggle with digital record-keeping, consider offering bookkeeping as an additional service. This ensures compliance while creating an additional revenue stream for your practice.

**Set boundaries:** Make clear in your engagement letters what you expect from clients in terms of record-keeping, and what falls outside your scope of responsibility.

Remember, Xero makes digital record-keeping straightforward with features like mobile receipt capture, bank feed automation, and intuitive interfaces that even non-accountants can navigate.

## 3. How do I handle errors or amendments after submitting a VAT return?

Even with careful review processes, errors happen. Perhaps a large invoice was coded incorrectly, or a client remembered a purchase after you'd already submitted their return. Understanding how to handle corrections is crucial.

For small errors (net value under £10,000), HMRC allows you to adjust the next VAT return rather than amending the original submission. This is the simplest approach and works well for most minor discrepancies.

For larger errors or those that would result in significant overpayment or underpayment of VAT, you'll need to notify HMRC separately using the VAT652 form.

**With Xero, managing corrections becomes more straightforward:**

- All transactions are timestamped, creating a clear audit trail
- You can easily identify when changes were made and by whom
- The system allows you to adjust subsequent returns while maintaining the historical record
- Notes and attachments can document why corrections were necessary

The most important thing is to address errors promptly and maintain transparent records of all corrections. This demonstrates professional diligence and protects both you and your clients in case of HMRC enquiries.
