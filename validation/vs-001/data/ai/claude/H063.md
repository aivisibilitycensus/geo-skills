# 10 Signs Your Legal SEO Specialist Sucks

If you're a law firm investing in search engine optimization, you deserve results. You're paying good money to attract potential clients through Google searches, but is your legal SEO specialist actually delivering, or are you throwing your marketing budget into a black hole?

Many attorneys aren't sure what to look for in an SEO provider. The technical jargon, constantly changing algorithms, and opaque reporting can make it difficult to know whether you're getting value for your investment. That's exactly what some mediocre (or downright terrible) SEO specialists count on.

At Clio, we've seen countless law firms struggle with ineffective SEO partnerships before finding solutions that actually work. We've identified the warning signs that separate exceptional legal SEO specialists from those who are wasting your time and money.

Here are ten red flags that indicate your legal SEO specialist isn't cutting it—and what you should expect instead.

## 1. They Promise Instant Results or Guaranteed #1 Rankings

Let's start with the most obvious warning sign: unrealistic promises.

If your SEO specialist guaranteed you'd rank #1 for "personal injury lawyer" in a major metropolitan area within 30 days, they're either lying or using black-hat tactics that will eventually get your website penalized by Google.

Legitimate SEO is a long-term strategy. Quality legal SEO typically takes 3-6 months before you see meaningful results, and sometimes longer depending on your market's competitiveness and your starting point. Anyone promising overnight success is selling snake oil.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They set realistic expectations, explain the timeline for different types of results, and provide milestone-based goals. They'll discuss the competitive landscape in your practice area and geographic market honestly, helping you understand what's achievable and when.

## 2. You Have No Idea What They're Actually Doing

Does your monthly SEO report look like it was generated by throwing random numbers at a template? Do you receive invoices with vague line items like "SEO services" without any detail about the actual work performed?

Opacity is a major red flag. You're paying for a service—you deserve to know exactly what you're getting.

Your legal SEO specialist should be able to clearly explain their strategy in terms you understand. If they hide behind jargon and refuse to break down their activities into comprehensible tasks, they're either incompetent or they're doing very little actual work.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They provide detailed monthly reports showing specific tasks completed, content created, links acquired, technical issues resolved, and performance metrics. They're happy to jump on calls to explain their work and answer questions in plain English.

## 3. They're Using Outdated or Black-Hat Tactics

SEO has evolved dramatically over the past decade. Tactics that worked in 2010 not only don't work today—they can actively harm your website's rankings.

Red flags include:
- Keyword stuffing (unnaturally cramming keywords into content)
- Buying links from link farms or sketchy directories
- Creating dozens of location-specific pages with duplicate content
- Hiding text or links on your website
- Building a network of low-quality microsites pointing to your main site

These shortcuts might show temporary gains, but Google's algorithms will eventually catch up and penalize your site. Recovering from a Google penalty can take months or even years.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They focus on white-hat tactics that align with Google's guidelines: creating genuinely helpful content, earning legitimate backlinks from reputable sources, optimizing technical SEO, and building your online authority naturally over time.

## 4. They Don't Understand the Legal Industry

Not all SEO is created equal. Legal SEO has unique challenges and opportunities that require specialized knowledge.

Your SEO specialist should understand:
- Attorney advertising rules and ethics requirements in your jurisdiction
- The client journey for legal services (which is often much longer than other industries)
- Legal-specific keywords and search intent
- The importance of demonstrating expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness (E-A-T) for legal content
- How different practice areas attract different types of searches

If your SEO provider treats your law firm exactly like they treat a restaurant or plumbing company, they're missing critical nuances that could make or break your campaign.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They specialize in legal marketing or have extensive experience working with law firms. They understand your competitive landscape, speak your language, and know the specific regulations governing attorney advertising.

## 5. Your Website Traffic Is Growing, But You're Not Getting Qualified Leads

Vanity metrics can be deceiving. Sure, your organic traffic increased 300%—but if those visitors aren't potential clients, who cares?

A bad SEO specialist might drive completely irrelevant traffic to your site, inflating the numbers in their reports while delivering zero actual value. They might optimize for keywords that get searches but don't indicate any intention to hire an attorney.

For example, ranking highly for "how to file divorce papers yourself" might bring traffic, but those searchers are specifically looking to avoid hiring a lawyer. That's not helpful for your family law practice.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They focus on intent-driven keywords that indicate someone is looking for legal representation. They track not just traffic, but qualified leads, consultation requests, and ultimately, client acquisition. They understand the difference between informational and transactional searches and optimize for the right mix.

## 6. They Never Talk About Your Competitors

SEO doesn't exist in a vacuum. Your rankings depend not just on what you do, but also on what your competitors are doing.

If your SEO specialist never mentions your competition or hasn't done a competitive analysis, they're flying blind. They can't develop an effective strategy without understanding who you're competing against and what's working for them.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They perform thorough competitive analyses, identifying who ranks for your target keywords and why. They look for gaps in your competitors' strategies that you can exploit and find opportunities they've missed. They keep you informed about major moves by key competitors and adjust your strategy accordingly.

## 7. Content Creation Is an Afterthought (or Non-Existent)

"Content is king" has become a cliché, but it remains fundamentally true—especially for legal SEO.

Google wants to rank websites that provide valuable, authoritative information. For law firms, that means creating content that genuinely helps people understand their legal issues and demonstrates your expertise.

If your SEO specialist isn't regularly publishing high-quality blog posts, practice area pages, FAQs, and other content, they're missing one of the most powerful tools in the SEO toolkit. And if they're publishing thin, generic content that could apply to any law firm, that's almost as bad as publishing nothing at all.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They develop a content strategy aligned with your practice areas and target client questions. They create (or help you create) in-depth, authoritative content that showcases your expertise. They understand that lawyers are busy and either provide content creation services or guide you on the most impactful content to create yourself.

## 8. They Ignore Technical SEO Issues

Great content won't help if Google can't properly crawl and index your website. Technical SEO forms the foundation that everything else builds upon.

Common technical issues that bad SEO specialists ignore:
- Slow page load times
- Mobile usability problems
- Broken links and 404 errors
- Duplicate content issues
- Poor site architecture
- Missing or poorly configured XML sitemaps
- HTTPS security problems
- Structured data markup errors

If your SEO specialist has never mentioned technical SEO or hasn't audited your website for these issues, they're not doing their job.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They conduct comprehensive technical SEO audits and prioritize fixing critical issues. They monitor your site's technical health continuously and address problems as they arise. They ensure your website provides an excellent user experience on all devices.

## 9. They're Not Tracking or Reporting on Meaningful Metrics

Which would you rather know: that your website had 10,000 visitors last month, or that you received 50 consultation requests from people searching for lawyers in your practice area?

Bad SEO specialists focus on surface-level metrics that sound impressive but don't connect to your business goals. They might report on keyword rankings, traffic numbers, and bounce rates without ever discussing what really matters: Are you getting more qualified leads? Are you acquiring more clients? What's your return on investment?

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They tie SEO metrics to business outcomes. They track form submissions, phone calls from organic search, consultation bookings, and work with you to understand which leads convert to paying clients. They help you calculate the ROI of your SEO investment and make data-driven decisions about where to focus efforts.

## 10. Communication Is Poor or Non-Existent

Remember when you signed that contract and your SEO specialist was super responsive, answering questions promptly and enthusiastically? Now, months later, you can barely get them to return an email?

Poor communication is more than just annoying—it's a sign that you're not a priority for them. Maybe they've over-extended themselves with too many clients, or maybe they're simply disorganized and unprofessional.

Either way, you deserve better. SEO is an ongoing partnership, and your specialist should be accessible when you have questions or concerns.

**What good SEO specialists do instead:** They maintain regular communication through scheduled check-ins, respond to emails within a reasonable timeframe, and are proactive about alerting you to issues or opportunities. They're available when you need them and treat you like a valued partner, not just another account.

## What to Do If Your Legal SEO Specialist Sucks

If you've recognized your current provider in several of these warning signs, you have some decisions to make.

**First, communicate your concerns.** Sometimes the problem is fixable. Schedule a candid conversation about what's not working and what you expect. Give them a chance to address the issues.

**Second, review your contract.** Understand your obligations and what's required to end the relationship if necessary.

**Third, don't let a bad experience sour you on SEO entirely.** When done right, legal SEO is one of the most cost-effective ways to grow your practice. The problem isn't SEO itself—it's your current provider.

**Finally, do your homework before hiring a replacement.** Ask detailed questions about their approach, request case studies from other law firms they've worked with, and get clear answers about reporting, communication, and expected results.
