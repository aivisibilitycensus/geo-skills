# How to Start a Non-Profit in Alaska

Starting a non-profit organization in Alaska can be a rewarding way to make a lasting impact on your community. Whether you're passionate about wildlife conservation, supporting Alaska Native communities, addressing homelessness, or promoting the arts, establishing a non-profit allows you to formalize your mission and access funding opportunities that can amplify your work.

While the process requires careful planning and attention to legal requirements, understanding the steps involved will help you navigate the journey successfully. Here's what you need to know about starting a non-profit in Alaska.

## Understanding Non-Profit Organizations

Before diving into the formation process, it's important to understand what makes an organization a non-profit. Non-profit organizations are entities created to serve public or mutual benefit rather than to generate profit for owners or shareholders. Any revenue generated must be reinvested into the organization's mission rather than distributed to members or directors.

In Alaska, most non-profits are formed as corporations under state law and apply for tax-exempt status under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. This designation allows donors to make tax-deductible contributions and exempts the organization from most federal and state taxes.

## Develop Your Mission and Vision

The foundation of any successful non-profit is a clear, compelling mission. Take time to articulate exactly what problem you're addressing and how your organization will make a difference. Your mission statement should be concise yet powerful enough to inspire donors, volunteers, and community members.

Consider conducting research to ensure there's a genuine need for your services and that you're not duplicating efforts of existing organizations. If similar non-profits exist, think about how you can collaborate or differentiate your approach.

## Choose Your Board of Directors

Alaska law requires non-profit corporations to have at least one director, though the IRS recommends at least three unrelated board members for 501(c)(3) organizations. Your board will be responsible for governance, strategic planning, and ensuring the organization fulfills its mission.

Select individuals who bring diverse skills, connections, and expertise to your organization. Look for people passionate about your cause who can contribute meaningfully through fundraising, financial oversight, legal knowledge, or community connections. Remember that board members cannot receive compensation for their service, though they may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses.

## File Articles of Incorporation

To legally establish your non-profit in Alaska, you must file Articles of Incorporation with the Alaska Division of Corporations, Business and Professional Licensing. This document officially creates your corporation and includes:

- Your organization's name (which must include "Corporation," "Company," "Incorporated," or an abbreviation)
- Your registered agent and office address in Alaska
- Your organization's purpose
- Information about your board of directors
- Dissolution provisions explaining how assets will be distributed if the organization closes

The filing fee is currently $50 for domestic non-profit corporations. You can file online through the state's business portal or by mail.

## Create Your Bylaws

Bylaws are the internal rules that govern how your non-profit operates. While not filed with the state, bylaws are essential for organizational structure and are required when applying for tax-exempt status. Your bylaws should address:

- Board composition, roles, and meeting procedures
- Officer positions and responsibilities
- Membership structure (if applicable)
- Conflict of interest policies
- Amendment procedures
- Fiscal year and financial management

Consider consulting with an attorney to ensure your bylaws comply with Alaska law and IRS requirements.

## Obtain Your EIN

Every non-profit needs an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS, which functions like a Social Security number for your organization. You'll need this to open a bank account, hire employees, and apply for tax-exempt status. You can obtain an EIN immediately by applying online through the IRS website at no cost.

## Apply for 501(c)(3) Tax-Exempt Status

To receive federal tax-exempt status, you must file Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ with the IRS. This application requires detailed information about your organization's activities, finances, governance, and compensation practices.

Organizations expecting gross receipts under $50,000 annually may qualify for the simplified Form 1023-EZ, which has a lower filing fee ($275 versus $600). However, the full Form 1023 provides stronger documentation of your exempt status.

Processing times vary, but expect to wait several months for IRS review. During this period, you can generally operate as a non-profit, though donations won't be tax-deductible until approval is granted (though the IRS typically makes exemptions retroactive to your filing date).

## Register with Alaska

Once you receive federal tax-exempt status, register with the Alaska Department of Revenue to obtain state tax exemption. You may also need to register for charitable solicitation if you plan to fundraise from the public.
