# Manufacturing TA Gold: Rethinking the Hiring Process with Carolyn Boise of American Iron and Metal

Finding and keeping great talent in manufacturing has always been challenging. But in today's competitive labor market, companies that stick to outdated hiring practices are falling behind. Carolyn Boise, talent acquisition leader at American Iron and Metal (AIM), shares how modernizing the recruitment process can transform your ability to attract top manufacturing talent.

## The Manufacturing Talent Crisis Is Real

The manufacturing sector faces a unique recruitment challenge. With an aging workforce, evolving skill requirements, and persistent misconceptions about manufacturing careers, companies are struggling to fill critical roles. Traditional hiring approaches—posting jobs and waiting for applications—simply don't cut it anymore.

American Iron and Metal, a leader in metal recycling with operations across North America, recognized this reality and decided to take action. Rather than accepting talent scarcity as inevitable, they reimagined their entire hiring process from the ground up.

## Speed Matters More Than You Think

One of Carolyn's key insights? In manufacturing recruitment, speed wins. Top candidates for skilled positions are often off the market within days, not weeks. If your hiring process takes months to move from application to offer, you're losing the best people to faster-moving competitors.

AIM streamlined their process by eliminating unnecessary steps and empowering hiring managers to make quicker decisions. This meant implementing technology that could handle initial screenings, coordinate interviews seamlessly, and keep candidates engaged throughout the journey.

## The Candidate Experience Makes or Breaks You

Here's a truth many manufacturing companies overlook: your hiring process is a preview of what it's like to work at your company. A clunky, impersonal application process signals that you don't value people's time or experience.

Carolyn emphasizes creating a candidate experience that reflects AIM's values and culture. This includes mobile-friendly applications (because many manufacturing workers are applying on their phones), clear communication at every stage, and treating every applicant with respect—whether they get the job or not.

Remember: today's rejected candidate could be tomorrow's perfect hire, or they might know someone who is. Word of mouth in manufacturing communities is powerful.

## Technology as an Enabler, Not a Replacement

One common mistake is thinking technology will solve all hiring problems automatically. Carolyn's approach is different: use technology to eliminate administrative burdens so recruiters can focus on what really matters—building relationships with candidates.

The right recruiting platform can automate job postings across multiple channels, screen for basic qualifications, and provide analytics on what's working. But technology should enhance human judgment, not replace it. The personal touch remains essential, especially when you're asking someone to make a career decision.

## Building Talent Pipelines, Not Just Filling Positions

Perhaps the most significant shift in AIM's approach is moving from reactive to proactive recruitment. Rather than scrambling when a position opens, they maintain ongoing relationships with potential candidates—even when there's no immediate opening.

This pipeline approach means hosting open houses, engaging with trade schools and apprenticeship programs, and staying connected with strong candidates who weren't the right fit for a previous role. When a position does open, you already have interested, qualified people to reach out to.

## The Bottom Line for Your Business

Rethinking your hiring process isn't just about filling positions faster—though that's certainly valuable. It's about building a competitive advantage through better talent. In manufacturing, where skilled workers directly impact productivity, quality, and safety, getting hiring right affects your bottom line.

The companies that will thrive are those willing to challenge old assumptions and invest in modern, candidate-focused recruitment strategies. As Carolyn Boise's work at American Iron and Metal demonstrates, the gold in manufacturing TA comes from treating recruitment as a strategic priority, not an administrative task.

Is your hiring process helping you win the war for manufacturing talent, or holding you back?
