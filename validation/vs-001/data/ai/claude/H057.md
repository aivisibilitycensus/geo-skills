# March 2022: The Small Business Owner's HR, Benefits, Payroll and Tax Compliance Deadlines

Running a small business means juggling countless responsibilities, and staying on top of compliance deadlines is one of the most critical—and often most stressful—aspects of business ownership. Missing important HR, benefits, payroll, or tax deadlines can result in costly penalties, damaged employee relationships, and unnecessary legal complications.

As we navigate through March 2022, we've compiled this comprehensive guide to help you stay compliant and avoid potential pitfalls. Whether you're managing payroll in-house or working with a professional employer organization (PEO), understanding these deadlines will help you plan your month effectively and ensure your business runs smoothly.

## Key Payroll Tax Deadlines for March 2022

### March 15, 2022: S-Corporation and Partnership Tax Returns Due

If your business operates as an S-corporation or partnership, March 15th is a critical date on your calendar. This is the deadline for filing Form 1120-S (for S-corporations) or Form 1065 (for partnerships) for the 2021 tax year.

These returns report your business income, deductions, and the allocation of profits and losses to shareholders or partners. Even if you're planning to file for an extension, you must submit Form 7004 by this date to receive an automatic six-month extension to September 15, 2022.

**Important considerations:**
- Extensions provide more time to file but don't extend the time to pay any taxes owed
- K-1 forms must be issued to shareholders or partners showing their share of income, deductions, and credits
- Late filing can result in penalties of $205 per shareholder/partner per month (or part of a month) that the return is late

### March 15, 2022: Monthly Payroll Tax Deposits (February 2022)

If you're a monthly depositor under the IRS payroll tax deposit schedule, your February 2022 payroll tax deposits are due by March 15th. This includes federal income tax withholding, Social Security, and Medicare taxes (FICA).

Your deposit schedule depends on the total tax liability you reported during a specific lookback period. If you reported $50,000 or less in taxes during the lookback period, you're typically classified as a monthly depositor.

**Best practices:**
- Use the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS) to ensure timely deposits
- Keep detailed records of all deposits made
- Review your deposit schedule annually as it may change based on your tax liability

### Ongoing: Semi-Weekly Payroll Tax Deposits

If your business has a larger payroll tax liability (more than $50,000 during the lookback period), you're classified as a semi-weekly depositor. This means you must deposit employment taxes based on when you pay wages:

- For wages paid Wednesday through Friday: Deposit by the following Wednesday
- For wages paid Saturday through Tuesday: Deposit by the following Friday

Mark your calendar throughout March to ensure these deposits are made on time. The penalties for late deposits can be substantial, ranging from 2% to 15% of the unpaid tax, depending on how late the payment is.

## Critical Benefits and ACA Compliance Deadlines

### March 2, 2022: ACA Forms 1095-C and 1095-B Distribution Deadline

While the IRS extended the deadline for filing Forms 1094-C and 1095-C to the IRS until April 1, 2022 (for paper filing) or May 31, 2022 (for electronic filing), the deadline for distributing Form 1095-C to employees was March 2, 2022.

If you're an Applicable Large Employer (ALE)—generally, employers with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees—you must provide Form 1095-C to each full-time employee. This form reports the health insurance coverage you offered and whether the employee enrolled.

**Key points to remember:**
- Even with the extended filing deadline to the IRS, employee copies should have been distributed by March 2nd
- Penalties for late distribution to employees can be $280 per form (for 2022)
- If you missed this deadline, distribute forms immediately to minimize penalties

### Ongoing: FSA and HSA Compliance

March is an excellent time to review your Flexible Spending Account (FSA) and Health Savings Account (HSA) administration for compliance:

**FSA Considerations:**
- Employees have until March 15, 2022 (or later, depending on your plan year) to incur eligible expenses for the 2021 plan year if you offer a grace period
- Review claims submissions and ensure proper documentation
- Communicate remaining balances to employees before funds expire

**HSA Considerations:**
- Ensure contributions don't exceed 2022 limits: $3,650 for individual coverage, $7,300 for family coverage, plus $1,000 catch-up for those 55 and older
- Verify that employees enrolled in HSAs are in high-deductible health plans (HDHPs)
- Provide employees with information about their contribution limits and tax benefits

### COBRA Continuation Coverage

If you have 20 or more employees, March is a good time to audit your COBRA administration. Ensure that:

- Qualifying event notices are being sent within required timeframes (44 days for employer-initiated events)
- Election notices are provided within 14 days of receiving notification of a qualifying event
- Premium payments are being processed correctly
- Coverage is being terminated appropriately for non-payment

COBRA violations can result in significant penalties, including excise taxes of $100 per day per affected individual, so maintaining compliance is essential.

## HR Compliance and Reporting Requirements

### Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO-1) Preparation

While the 2022 EEO-1 Component 1 report (covering 2021 data) typically isn't due until later in the spring, March is an ideal time to begin preparing if you're a private employer with 100 or more employees, or a federal contractor with 50 or more employees.

The EEO-1 report collects workforce data categorized by race/ethnicity, gender, and job category. Starting your preparation now will help you:

- Ensure data accuracy
- Identify and correct any discrepancies in employee records
- Make the filing process less stressful when the deadline arrives

### Form I-9 Internal Audit

March is an excellent time to conduct an internal audit of your Form I-9 records. All U.S. employers must complete and retain Form I-9 for each employee hired after November 6, 1986.

**Audit checklist:**
- Verify that all current employees have I-9s on file
- Ensure Section 1 was completed on or before the employee's first day of work
- Confirm that Section 2 was completed within three business days of the employee's start date
- Check that reverification was completed for employees with temporary work authorization
- Review forms for common errors like missing signatures, incorrect dates, or incomplete information

Regular self-audits help you identify and correct issues before a government inspection occurs. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) can impose fines ranging from hundreds to thousands of dollars per form for violations.

### Workers' Compensation Insurance Compliance

Most states require employers to carry workers' compensation insurance. March is a good time to:

- Verify that your coverage is current and adequate for your workforce size
- Review your industry classification codes to ensure you're properly rated
- Confirm that all employees are covered under your policy
- Check that required posters are displayed in the workplace

Workers' compensation requirements vary significantly by state, and operating without required coverage can result in severe penalties, including criminal charges in some jurisdictions.

## Employee Benefits Communication and Open Enrollment Planning

### Health Savings Account (HSA) Education

With tax season in full swing, March is an opportune time to educate employees about HSA tax advantages. Many employees don't maximize their HSA contributions because they don't fully understand the benefits.

**Key messages to share:**
- HSA contributions are tax-deductible (or pre-tax if made through payroll)
- Funds grow tax-free
- Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free
- Unlike FSAs, HSA funds roll over year after year
- HSAs are portable—employees keep their accounts even if they leave your company

Providing clear communication about HSAs can increase employee satisfaction and help your team make better financial decisions.

### Retirement Plan Contributions and Compliance

If you offer a 401(k) or other retirement plan, March involves several important considerations:

**Employee Contribution Limits:**
Remind employees who maxed out their 2021 contributions that the 2022 limits have increased:
- 401(k), 403(b), and most 457 plans: $20,500 (up from $19,500 in 2021)
- Catch-up contributions for those 50 and older: $6,500
- SIMPLE IRA: $14,000 (up from $13,500 in 2021)
- Catch-up contributions for SIMPLE: $3,000

**Employer Responsibilities:**
- Ensure timely deposit of employee deferrals (as soon as administratively feasible, generally within 7 business days for small plans)
- Review plan documents for any required amendments based on legislation changes
- Prepare for annual discrimination testing (ADP/ACP tests)
- Verify that your plan is operating according to its written document

### Planning for Benefits Open Enrollment

If your benefits plan year begins July 1 or later, March is the time to start planning your open enrollment period. Early planning ensures:

- Sufficient time to negotiate with insurance carriers
- Ability to compare multiple options and secure competitive rates
- Opportunity to survey employees about their benefits preferences
- Adequate time to prepare enrollment materials and communications
- Proper training for HR staff on plan changes

A well-organized open enrollment process reduces errors, improves employee understanding, and increases benefits utilization.

## State-Specific Compliance Considerations

### State Unemployment Insurance (SUI) Taxes

Most states require quarterly unemployment insurance tax filings. While the first quarter of 2022 doesn't end until March 31st, now is the time to ensure you're prepared for the April deadline (typically April 30th for most states).

**Preparation steps:**
- Verify that all new hires have been properly reported to your state workforce agency
- Reconcile wage records to ensure accuracy
- Review your SUI tax rate for 2022 (rates typically change annually)
- Ensure you're registered in all states where you have employees

Each state has its own unemployment insurance requirements, tax rates, and wage bases, making multi-state compliance particularly complex.

### State-Specific Paid Leave Programs

Several states have implemented paid family and medical leave programs with specific compliance requirements:

**States with active programs (as of March 2022):**
- California
- Connecticut
- District of Columbia
- Massachusetts
- New Jersey
- New York
- Oregon
- Rhode Island
- Washington

If you have employees in these jurisdictions, verify that:
- You're withholding the correct amount from employee wages (where applicable)
- You're making required employer contributions
- You're providing required notices to employees
- You're maintaining proper documentation

Additional states are implementing paid leave programs in 2022 and beyond, so staying informed about legislative changes is essential.

### State and Local Minimum Wage Increases

Many states and municipalities implemented minimum wage increases effective January 1, 2022. If you haven't already, March is the time to audit your payroll to ensure compliance:

- Verify that all employees are being paid at least the applicable minimum wage
- Review tipped employee compensation (different minimum wage rules often apply)
- Check that salary thresholds for exempt employees meet both federal and state requirements
- Ensure your payroll system is configured with the correct rates

Non-compliance with minimum wage laws can result in back pay obligations, penalties, and potential lawsuits.
