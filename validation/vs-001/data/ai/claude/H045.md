## The Coronavirus Job Retention Scheme (Furlough)

One of the most widely used support measures was the Coronavirus Job Retention Scheme, commonly known as the furlough scheme. This programme allowed employers to claim grants covering a significant portion of their employees' wages if those employees couldn't work due to coronavirus restrictions.

### How the Furlough Scheme Worked

Under the scheme, employers could claim up to 80% of an employee's usual salary for hours not worked, capped at £2,500 per month. The scheme evolved over time, with the government initially covering the full amount, before introducing flexible furlough and eventually requiring employers to contribute to wages and National Insurance.

The scheme was available to all UK employers with a PAYE payroll scheme that was created and started on or before 19 March 2020. This included businesses of all sizes, charities, recruitment agencies, and public authorities.

To claim, employers needed to:

- Designate affected employees as furloughed workers and submit information to HMRC
- Keep accurate records of furloughed employees and their usual working hours
- Make a claim through the HMRC online portal
- Maintain detailed financial records to support claims

## Self-Employment Income Support Scheme (SEISS)

While the furlough scheme supported employees, the Self-Employment Income Support Scheme was designed specifically for self-employed individuals and partnerships whose business had been affected by coronavirus.

### Eligibility and Claim Process

To qualify for SEISS, you needed to:

- Be self-employed or a member of a partnership
- Have submitted your 2018-19 tax return by 23 April 2020
- Have traded in both tax years 2018-19 and 2019-20
- Intend to continue trading in 2020-21
- Have lost trading profits due to COVID-19
- Have trading profits of no more than £50,000 (and at least equal to your non-trading income)

The scheme provided grants calculated as a percentage of average trading profits over specific tax years. The first and second grants covered 80% of three months' average profits (capped at £7,500), while later grants offered varying percentages and caps depending on how severely your business was affected.

Unlike many other schemes, SEISS claims were made directly through HMRC's online system. If you were eligible, HMRC contacted you directly with information about when and how to claim. This made record-keeping absolutely essential—you needed accurate tax returns and profit calculations, which is where comprehensive accounting software proves invaluable.

## Bounce Back Loans

The Bounce Back Loan Scheme offered a quick and accessible way for small and medium-sized businesses to access funds between £2,000 and £50,000. These government-backed loans provided vital cash flow support with favourable terms.

### Key Features

- Loans from 25% to 100% of your average turnover (capped at £50,000)
- No interest or repayments for the first 12 months
- Fixed interest rate of 2.5% per year after the first year
- Repayment terms of up to 10 years
- 100% government guarantee, meaning no personal guarantees or collateral required
- No fees for setting up the loan

The application process was streamlined through participating lenders, making it one of the fastest ways to access funding. The scheme closed to new applications on 31 March 2021, but businesses with existing Bounce Back Loans continue to repay under the agreed terms.

## Coronavirus Business Interruption Loan Scheme (CBILS)

For businesses needing larger amounts or more flexible terms than Bounce Back Loans offered, the Coronavirus Business Interruption Loan Scheme provided another option.

### What CBILS Offered

CBILS supported SMEs with access to loans, overdrafts, invoice finance, and asset finance of up to £5 million. The government guaranteed 80% of the finance to the lender, reducing their risk and making approval more accessible.

Key benefits included:

- No personal guarantees for facilities under £250,000
- The government paid interest and fees for the first 12 months
- Terms available up to six years for term loans and asset finance
- Up to three years for overdrafts and invoice finance

CBILS required more detailed financial information than Bounce Back Loans, including business plans and financial statements. Maintaining organised, up-to-date financial records throughout the year positioned businesses to apply quickly when needed.

## Business Rates Relief

For businesses operating from physical premises, business rates represent a significant overhead. The government introduced business rates holidays for specific sectors hit particularly hard by restrictions.

### Who Benefited

Retail, hospitality, and leisure properties in England received 100% business rates relief for the 2020-21 tax year, with further reliefs available in subsequent years. This meant eligible businesses paid no business rates for their properties during this period.

Nursery providers also received similar relief, recognising their crucial role in supporting working families and the challenges they faced during the pandemic.

This relief was applied automatically—businesses didn't need to apply. However, if you believed you qualified but didn't receive the relief, contacting your local authority was the appropriate next step.

## VAT Deferral and Payment Plans

To ease cash flow pressures, the government allowed businesses to defer VAT payments that became due between 20 March 2020 and 30 June 2020.

### Flexible Repayment Options

Rather than requiring a lump sum payment, businesses could opt into a payment plan spreading deferred VAT over up to 11 smaller monthly instalments. This arrangement was interest-free, providing breathing room for businesses rebuilding their cash reserves.

## Grants for Businesses

Beyond loans and deferrals, various grant schemes provided direct financial support that didn't need to be repaid.

### Types of Business Grants

**Small Business Grants:** Businesses in receipt of Small Business Rates Relief or Rural Rate Relief received grants of £10,000.

**Retail, Hospitality and Leisure Grants:** Businesses in these sectors with properties under a certain rateable value received grants of £10,000 or £25,000.

**Local Authority Discretionary Grants:** Local councils received funding to support businesses that didn't qualify for other grant schemes but still faced significant impacts—particularly those with high fixed costs but operating from shared spaces or without their own business rates assessment.

**Restart Grants:** As restrictions eased, one-off grants helped businesses reopen, with amounts varying based on property type and rateable value.

These grants were administered by local authorities, with application processes varying by location. Checking your local council's website provided specific information about what was available in your area.

## Making the Most of Financial Support

Understanding what support is available represents only the first step. Actually accessing these funds and using them effectively requires organisation and planning.

### Keep Impeccable Records

Whether claiming furlough support, applying for loans, or demonstrating eligibility for grants, you'll need detailed financial records. These include:

- Accurate profit and loss statements
- Tax returns filed on time
- Payroll records showing who was employed and when
- Evidence of how coronavirus impacted your trading
- Bank statements and cash flow projections

Modern accounting solutions like FreshBooks make maintaining these records straightforward, automatically categorising expenses, tracking income, generating reports, and keeping everything in one accessible place.

### Understand Your Obligations

Each support scheme came with specific terms and conditions. For furlough claims, you needed to ensure employees weren't working during furloughed hours. For loans, understanding repayment terms and what would happen if circumstances changed was essential. Grant funding sometimes came with restrictions on how funds could be used.

Taking time to read guidance carefully and seek professional advice when needed protected your business from complications later.

### Plan for Recovery

Financial support schemes provided a lifeline during the crisis, but sustainable recovery requires forward planning. Use the breathing room these schemes provided to:

- Reassess your business model for a changed marketplace
- Identify opportunities emerging from new consumer behaviours
- Build cash reserves for future uncertainty
- Streamline operations and reduce unnecessary costs
- Invest in digital capabilities that proved crucial during lockdowns

## Looking Ahead

While many COVID-19 support schemes have now closed to new applicants, understanding what was available helps businesses manage ongoing obligations like loan repayments. Some support measures continue in evolved forms, and the experience gained in accessing government support may prove valuable in future challenging times.

The pandemic reinforced the importance of financial resilience and adaptability. Businesses that maintained clear financial visibility throughout the crisis were better positioned to make informed decisions, access support quickly, and plan for recovery.
