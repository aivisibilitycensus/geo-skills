# ATS users: the more the merrier?

When it comes to implementing an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) in your organization, one question often comes up: how many team members should have access? While it might seem logical to limit users to keep things simple, the reality is that collaborative hiring leads to better outcomes. Let's explore why opening up your ATS to more users across your organization could be the key to building stronger teams.

## The traditional approach: keeping ATS access limited

Historically, many companies have restricted ATS access to HR professionals and recruiters. The reasoning seems sound enough—fewer users means less complexity, easier management, and lower costs. After all, why would someone from marketing or finance need to access your recruitment software?

This gatekeeping approach made sense when ATS platforms were clunky, difficult to use, and designed exclusively for recruitment specialists. However, this mindset creates bottlenecks that slow down your hiring process and isolate recruitment from the rest of your business strategy.

In today's competitive talent market, companies can't afford to operate in silos. The best hiring decisions happen when multiple perspectives come together, and your ATS should facilitate that collaboration, not hinder it.

## Why more users mean better hiring outcomes

### Diverse perspectives lead to stronger decisions

Hiring isn't just an HR function—it's a business-critical decision that affects every department. When you invite hiring managers, team leads, and even potential colleagues into the ATS, you create a more holistic evaluation process.

A marketing manager knows what skills their team needs far better than a recruiter working across multiple departments. A senior developer can spot technical expertise that might not be obvious from a resume alone. By giving these stakeholders direct access to your ATS, you enable them to review candidates, leave feedback, and participate meaningfully in the hiring process.

This collaborative approach reduces miscommunication and ensures that everyone involved in the hiring decision has the same information at their fingertips.

### Faster decision-making

Every day a position remains unfilled costs your company money and productivity. When hiring managers need to wait for HR to forward resumes, schedule feedback meetings, or consolidate interview notes, the process slows to a crawl.

With broader ATS access, stakeholders can review candidates in real-time, leave immediate feedback, and move qualified applicants through the pipeline without waiting for intermediaries. This acceleration is crucial when competing for top talent who often have multiple offers on the table.

### Improved candidate experience

Today's candidates expect a smooth, professional hiring process. When your team collaborates effectively within an ATS, candidates benefit from coordinated communication, timely updates, and well-organized interviews.

Nothing frustrates candidates more than being asked the same questions by multiple interviewers or experiencing long gaps in communication. A shared ATS ensures everyone knows what's been discussed, what questions have been asked, and what the next steps are—creating a seamless experience that strengthens your employer brand.

## Breaking down the concerns about adding users

### "Won't more users make things chaotic?"

This is a valid concern, but modern ATS platforms like Recruitee are designed specifically to handle multiple users without creating chaos. Through customizable permissions, you control exactly what each user can see and do.

For example, you might give hiring managers full access to candidates for their open positions while limiting access to other departments. Interviewers might be able to view candidate profiles and leave feedback without being able to change job postings or send communications.

This granular control means you get the benefits of collaboration without sacrificing organization or data security.

### "What about data privacy and security?"

Employee data requires careful handling, and adding users does increase the need for robust security measures. However, the solution isn't to limit access—it's to choose an ATS with proper security features and user permissions.

Quality ATS platforms offer role-based access controls, audit trails, and compliance features that protect sensitive information. When properly configured, these systems are actually more secure than the alternative: emailing candidate resumes and interview notes back and forth outside of a controlled environment.

### "Won't this increase costs significantly?"

Many modern ATS platforms have evolved beyond per-user pricing models that punish collaboration. When evaluating ATS options, look for pricing structures that encourage, rather than penalize, adding team members to the platform.

The cost of a few additional ATS users pales in comparison to the cost of a bad hire, which can range from 30% to 200% of the employee's annual salary according to various studies. If broader access helps you avoid even one hiring mistake, it pays for itself many times over.

## Who should have access to your ATS?

While "more users" doesn't necessarily mean "everyone in the company," it typically means expanding beyond the traditional HR-only model. Consider giving access to:

**Hiring managers** need comprehensive access to their open positions, from reviewing applications to scheduling interviews and providing feedback.

**Department heads** benefit from oversight of their team's recruitment efforts, even if they're not involved in day-to-day hiring activities.

**Interview panel members** should be able to access candidate information before interviews and submit structured feedback afterward.

**Executive leadership** often wants visibility into recruitment metrics and pipeline status, even without being involved in individual hiring decisions.

**Recruitment coordinators and assistants** need access to manage scheduling and communication without necessarily having full recruiting permissions.

## Making collaborative hiring work in practice

Simply adding users to your ATS isn't enough—you need to set up your system and processes for collaborative success.

**Establish clear roles and responsibilities.** Just because someone has ATS access doesn't mean they should be involved in every hiring decision. Define who does what in your recruitment process.

**Provide training.** Your ATS is only as effective as your team's ability to use it. Invest in onboarding for new users and ongoing training as features evolve.

**Create feedback templates.** Structured feedback ensures that input from multiple users is comparable and useful. Build templates that guide users toward providing actionable insights.

**Set response time expectations.** When multiple people are involved in hiring, everyone needs to know how quickly they should review candidates and provide feedback to keep the process moving.

## The verdict: collaboration wins

In today's hiring landscape, the companies that move fastest with the best-informed decisions win the talent war. An ATS that supports collaborative hiring gives you a significant competitive advantage.

Rather than viewing additional users as a complication, see them as force multipliers that bring diverse expertise, faster decision-making, and better candidate experiences to your recruitment process.

The question shouldn't be "how few users can we get away with?" but rather "who needs to be involved to make the best possible hiring decisions?" When you choose an ATS platform that makes collaboration easy rather than costly or complicated, the answer becomes clear: yes, more truly is merrier.
