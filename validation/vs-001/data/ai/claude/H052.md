# What Is Form 1120S, and How Do You File It?

If you run an S corporation, Form 1120S is one of the most important tax documents you'll deal with each year. While S corps offer significant tax advantages, they also come with specific filing requirements that can seem overwhelming at first glance.

Let's break down everything you need to know about Form 1120S, so you can approach tax season with confidence.

## What Is Form 1120S?

Form 1120S, officially known as the "U.S. Income Tax Return for an S Corporation," is the annual tax return that S corporations must file with the IRS. Unlike C corporations that pay corporate income tax, S corporations are pass-through entities. This means the business itself doesn't pay federal income tax. Instead, income, deductions, and credits pass through to shareholders, who report them on their personal tax returns.

Think of Form 1120S as the comprehensive report card for your S corporation's financial year. It details your company's income, expenses, deductions, credits, and each shareholder's portion of these items.

## Who Needs to File Form 1120S?

Any business that has elected S corporation status with the IRS must file Form 1120S annually. This includes:

- Corporations that filed Form 2553 (Election by a Small Business Corporation) and received IRS approval
- LLCs that have elected to be taxed as S corporations
- Eligible domestic corporations with no more than 100 shareholders

Even if your S corporation had no income or activity during the tax year, you're still required to file Form 1120S.

## When Is Form 1120S Due?

Form 1120S is due by the 15th day of the third month after the end of your tax year. For most S corporations operating on a calendar year, this means **March 15th**.

If you need more time, you can file Form 7004 to request an automatic six-month extension, pushing your deadline to September 15th. However, remember that an extension to file isn't an extension to pay—any taxes owed are still due by the original March 15th deadline.

## Key Components of Form 1120S

Form 1120S includes several important sections:

**Income and Deductions**: Report your gross receipts, cost of goods sold, and business expenses like salaries, rent, and advertising.

**Schedule K**: This crucial schedule summarizes all income, deductions, and credits that pass through to shareholders.

**Schedule K-1**: Each shareholder receives an individual Schedule K-1 showing their specific share of the corporation's income, deductions, and credits to report on their personal tax returns.

**Balance Sheets and Reconciliation**: You'll need to provide a balance sheet and reconcile your book income with the income reported on the return.

## How to File Form 1120S

Filing Form 1120S involves several steps:

1. **Gather financial records**: Compile your income statements, balance sheets, and expense documentation for the tax year.

2. **Complete the form**: Fill out Form 1120S with your corporation's financial information. Many businesses use tax software or work with accounting professionals for this step.

3. **Prepare Schedule K-1s**: Create individual K-1 forms for each shareholder showing their portion of pass-through items.

4. **Submit to the IRS**: File Form 1120S electronically or by mail, and distribute K-1s to all shareholders by the filing deadline.
