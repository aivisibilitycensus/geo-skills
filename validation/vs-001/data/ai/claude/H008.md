# Why Wildbit's Belief that Businesses are Imaginary Could Become the Future of Work

In an era where corporate culture often feels rigid and impersonal, one software company is challenging everything we think we know about how businesses should operate. Wildbit, the team behind popular tools like Postmark and Beanstalk, has built their entire philosophy around a radical idea: businesses are imaginary.

This isn't just philosophical musing—it's a practical framework that's reshaping how they work, make decisions, and serve their customers. And if you're looking for software partners who truly understand the human side of business, understanding Wildbit's philosophy might just change how you think about work too.

## What Does "Businesses are Imaginary" Actually Mean?

At first glance, the statement sounds absurd. Businesses have bank accounts, employees, products, and customers—how can they be imaginary?

Wildbit's insight is more nuanced. They recognize that while the *outcomes* of business are real, the entity itself is a social construct. A business is really just a group of people who've agreed to work together toward common goals. The moment we forget this—the moment we treat "the business" as something separate from the humans who comprise it—we start making decisions that prioritize an imaginary entity over real people.

This distinction matters profoundly in practice. When companies put "business needs" above human needs, they're essentially sacrificing real people for an abstraction. Wildbit flips this script: they build their business around the lives they want to live, not the other way around.

## How This Philosophy Translates to Better Products and Service

You might wonder: what does a company's internal philosophy have to do with you as a customer?

Everything, it turns out.

When a company recognizes that businesses are imaginary constructs made of real people, they approach product development and customer service differently. At Wildbit, this means:

**Sustainable development over growth-at-all-costs.** Rather than chasing unsustainable growth targets to satisfy investors or arbitrary benchmarks, Wildbit focuses on creating excellent products that genuinely solve problems. This means you're not dealing with a company that will pivot away from your needs, deprecate features suddenly, or sacrifice quality for expansion.

**Genuine customer relationships.** When you contact Wildbit's support, you're talking to people who aren't being squeezed by impossible metrics or pushed to upsell you constantly. Their team members have reasonable workloads and the autonomy to actually solve your problems, not just follow scripts.

**Long-term thinking.** Companies built on the "businesses are imaginary" philosophy aren't optimizing for next quarter's earnings. They're building for longevity, which means the tools you integrate into your workflow today will likely still be there—and well-maintained—years from now.

## The Traditional Business Model is Breaking Down

Wildbit's philosophy isn't emerging in a vacuum. We're witnessing a fundamental shift in how people think about work.

The traditional model—sacrifice your present life for future rewards, climbing a corporate ladder, separating "work you" from "real you"—is increasingly untenable. Burnout rates are skyrocketing. Employee engagement hovers at historic lows. The pandemic accelerated a reckoning that was already underway: people are no longer willing to subjugate their entire lives to an imaginary entity called "the company."

Meanwhile, customers are also changing. We're tired of being treated as "users" or "accounts" rather than people. We want to do business with companies that share our values, that treat their employees well, and that think beyond the next earnings call.

Wildbit recognized these trends early and built accordingly. What once seemed like an idealistic approach now looks increasingly like the future of sustainable business.

## What This Means for Work-Life Integration

One of Wildbit's most distinctive practices stems from their core philosophy: they design work around life, not life around work.

This includes policies like:

- **Four-day work weeks during summer months**
- **Generous vacation policies that people actually use**
- **Flexible schedules that accommodate real human needs**
- **Remote work options long before they were trendy**

But here's what matters to you as a customer: these aren't perks that create inefficiency. They create a team that's energized, creative, and committed. When you're working with Wildbit's products, you're benefiting from the output of people who aren't running on fumes.

Think about the software tools you use daily. How many were built by burned-out teams rushing to meet arbitrary deadlines? How does that affect the quality, thoughtfulness, and reliability of what you're using?

Companies that treat their businesses as more important than the people within them create products that reflect that misalignment. Companies like Wildbit create products that reflect careful thought, sustainable practices, and genuine care.

## The Ripple Effect: Better Business Practices Spread

When you choose to work with companies that embody these values, you're not just making a purchasing decision—you're voting with your dollars for a different kind of business ecosystem.

Every successful company that operates on human-centric principles proves that the traditional model isn't the only way. Wildbit's profitability and longevity demonstrate that you don't have to choose between treating people well and running a successful business.

As customers increasingly select partners based on values alignment, more companies will be incentivized to adopt similar approaches. Your choice of which tools to use and which companies to support has real influence on what the future of work looks like.

## Practical Benefits You'll Experience

Let's get concrete about what this philosophy means for your day-to-day experience:

**Stability and reliability.** Companies built on sustainable practices stick around. Your integrations won't suddenly break because a company pivoted or got acquired.

**Thoughtful feature development.** Rather than a bloated product that tries to be everything to everyone, you get focused tools that solve specific problems exceptionally well.

**Responsive support.** When issues arise, you're dealing with empowered team members who have the time and authority to help, not overworked employees reading from scripts.

**Transparent communication.** Companies that see themselves clearly—as groups of people, not abstract entities—tend to communicate more honestly about challenges, changes, and decisions.

**Aligned incentives.** You're not being pushed toward the most expensive tier or unnecessary add-ons. You're offered solutions that actually fit your needs.

## The Challenge: Scaling Human-Centric Business

Of course, Wildbit's approach raises questions. Can this philosophy scale? Does it only work for small companies or specific industries?

These are fair questions, and the answers are still being written. What we know so far is encouraging: Wildbit has sustained this approach for years while remaining profitable and growing. They're proof of concept that human-centric business isn't just idealistic—it's viable.

The real question isn't whether this can work, but whether more companies will choose to work this way. As workers and customers increasingly demand better, that choice is becoming less optional.

## Why This Matters for Your Business

If you're reading this, you're likely making decisions about which tools and partners to integrate into your workflows. Beyond features and pricing, consider the philosophy behind the companies you're evaluating.

Companies that recognize businesses are imaginary—that they're really just collections of people—tend to build different kinds of relationships with their customers. They're partners, not vendors. They're in it for the long haul, not the quick win.

In an uncertain business landscape, partnering with companies that have sustainable, human-centric practices isn't just an ethical choice—it's a strategic one.

## Conclusion: The Future is Human

Wildbit's belief that businesses are imaginary isn't a rejection of commerce or profitability. It's a recognition of reality: businesses are made of people, and when we honor that truth, we build better products, better workplaces, and better outcomes for everyone involved.

As we move into an uncertain future, companies built on these principles offer something increasingly rare: stability rooted in sustainability, innovation rooted in humanity, and business relationships rooted in genuine partnership.

The future of work doesn't have to mean choosing between professional success and personal wellbeing. Companies like Wildbit are proving there's another way—one that might not just be the future, but the key to building businesses that actually last.
