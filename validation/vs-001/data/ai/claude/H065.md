# What Is a General Partnership?

If you're considering starting a business with one or more partners, understanding the general partnership structure is essential. This business arrangement is one of the simplest and most common ways to form a company with others, but it comes with specific advantages and considerations you should know about before moving forward.

## Defining a General Partnership

A general partnership is a business structure where two or more individuals agree to share ownership, responsibilities, profits, and liabilities of a business. Unlike corporations or limited liability companies (LLCs), general partnerships are relatively informal and straightforward to establish. In many cases, a partnership can form simply through an agreement between parties—sometimes even a verbal one, though we always recommend written documentation.

In a general partnership, all partners participate in the day-to-day management of the business and share equally in decision-making unless otherwise specified in a partnership agreement.

## How General Partnerships Work

When you form a general partnership, each partner contributes something to the business—whether that's money, property, labor, or expertise. In return, each partner receives a share of the business's profits and losses. By default, partnerships split everything equally among partners, but you can customize these arrangements through a partnership agreement.

All partners have the authority to make business decisions and enter into contracts on behalf of the partnership. This shared authority means that actions taken by one partner can bind the entire partnership, making trust and clear communication absolutely critical.

## Key Advantages of General Partnerships

**Simplicity and Low Cost**: General partnerships are easy and inexpensive to form. There's typically minimal paperwork compared to corporations or LLCs, and you won't face the same regulatory requirements or filing fees.

**Shared Responsibilities**: Running a business is demanding, and having partners means you can divide the workload. Partners can complement each other's skills, with one focusing on operations while another handles marketing or finances.

**Tax Benefits**: General partnerships enjoy "pass-through" taxation, meaning the business itself doesn't pay income taxes. Instead, profits and losses pass through to individual partners, who report them on their personal tax returns, avoiding the double taxation that corporations sometimes face.

## Important Considerations and Risks

**Unlimited Personal Liability**: This is the most significant drawback of a general partnership. Each partner has unlimited personal liability for the business's debts and obligations. If the business faces a lawsuit or can't pay its debts, creditors can pursue your personal assets—including your home, car, and savings.

**Joint and Several Liability**: You're not only responsible for your own actions but also for your partners' business decisions. If your partner makes a costly mistake or incurs debt on behalf of the partnership, you're equally liable.

**Potential for Disagreements**: Without clear agreements in place, partnerships can suffer from conflicts over decision-making, profit distribution, or business direction.

## Is a General Partnership Right for You?

A general partnership might be suitable if you're starting a low-risk business venture with people you trust implicitly. It works well for professional service providers, consultants, or small businesses just getting started.

However, if your business involves significant liability risks or you're uncomfortable with unlimited personal exposure, you might want to consider alternative structures like an LLC or limited partnership.

## Moving Forward

Before establishing any business partnership, we strongly recommend consulting with legal and financial professionals who can help you understand your options and create a comprehensive partnership agreement. This document should outline profit sharing, decision-making processes, dispute resolution procedures, and exit strategies.
