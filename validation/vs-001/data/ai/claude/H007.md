# The Next Evolution of Transparent Salaries: Our New Remote-First Formula and Updated Salary Calculator

In 2018, we made a bold commitment: complete salary transparency. We published our salary formula, opened our compensation calculator to the public, and invited the world to see exactly how we determine what every team member earns. It was uncomfortable at first, but it fundamentally changed how we think about fairness, equity, and value.

Today, we're taking the next step in that journey.

The world of work has transformed dramatically since we first introduced our transparent salary model. Remote work has shifted from a perk to a permanent reality. Geographic arbitrage has become a legitimate career strategy. And the conversation around fair compensation has evolved far beyond simple cost-of-living adjustments.

We've spent the past year rethinking our approach to compensation in this new reality, and we're excited to share what we've built: a remote-first salary formula that acknowledges the complexity of modern work while staying true to our commitment to radical transparency.

## Why Salary Transparency Still Matters

Before we dive into the specifics of our new formula, it's worth revisiting why we believe salary transparency is non-negotiable.

Traditional salary negotiation perpetuates inequality. Study after study shows that women and underrepresented minorities receive lower salary offers for identical work, often because they're less likely to negotiate aggressively or because unconscious bias influences hiring managers' initial offers. When salaries are opaque and negotiable, privilege compounds.

Transparency eliminates this problem at its root. When everyone knows the formula, there's no room for bias in salary determination. Two people doing the same role with the same experience receive the same compensation. It's simple, fair, and defensible.

But transparency does something else that's perhaps even more valuable: it builds trust. When prospective customers evaluate whether to work with us, they're not just assessing our products or services—they're evaluating our values. Companies that hide how they compensate their people often hide other things too. Transparency in one area signals transparency across the board.

For our team members, knowing they're compensated fairly means they can focus entirely on doing great work rather than wondering if their colleague negotiated a better deal. For our customers, it means working with a company that lives its values in every decision.

## The Challenge of Remote-First Compensation

Our original salary formula included a geographic multiplier based on the cost of labor in different markets. Someone working in San Francisco received a different multiplier than someone in Nashville, reflecting the reality that talent costs more in certain locations.

This made sense in a world where people worked in offices and genuinely lived in the cities where we operated. But the remote work revolution exposed significant problems with this approach.

First, geographic-based pay created perverse incentives. Team members could move to lower-cost areas and take significant pay cuts through no fault of their own, effectively penalizing lifestyle choices that had no bearing on their work quality. Alternatively, some companies that paid based on employee location inadvertently encouraged people to lie about where they lived—not exactly the foundation for a trust-based relationship.

Second, geographic pay multipliers often reflected outdated assumptions about value. Does an engineer in San Francisco genuinely deliver more value than an equally skilled engineer in Portugal? Not in a truly remote environment where both are working asynchronously and judged on output rather than presence.

Third, we recognized that "location-based pay" was often a euphemism for paying people less because we could, not because their work was worth less. The global talent pool means we can hire excellent people everywhere, but that shouldn't mean we pay them less simply because their local cost of living is lower.

Finally, we had to confront an uncomfortable truth: if we truly believed in equal pay for equal work, how could we justify paying people differently based on where they happened to live?

## Introducing Our Remote-First Formula

After extensive research, countless spreadsheet iterations, and honest conversations with our entire team, we've developed a new approach that we believe better reflects the reality of modern work.

Our updated formula has four components:

**Base Compensation × Role Level × Experience Multiplier × Performance Factor = Total Salary**

Let's break down each component.

### Base Compensation

Every role family (engineering, design, customer success, marketing, etc.) has a base compensation figure. This represents the fair market value for someone with fundamental competency in that role, working remotely.

We determine base compensation by analyzing salary data from multiple sources, focusing specifically on remote-first companies and global salary benchmarks rather than location-specific data. We target the 75th percentile of remote market rates, meaning we aim to pay better than 75% of comparable remote positions.

This is a significant philosophical shift. Rather than asking "what's the minimum we need to pay to hire someone in this location," we're asking "what does excellent talent cost in a global remote market?"

### Role Level

Not all positions within a role family are equal. A senior engineer provides more value than a junior engineer, not because they work more hours, but because they solve harder problems, mentor others, and operate more autonomously.

Our role levels are clearly defined with public rubrics. Each level has specific expectations around technical skill, scope of influence, autonomy, and leadership. These rubrics aren't subjective—they're based on observable behaviors and outcomes.

Role levels carry multipliers:
- Entry Level: 0.7x
- Junior: 0.85x
- Mid-Level: 1.0x
- Senior: 1.3x
- Staff/Lead: 1.6x
- Principal: 2.0x

These multipliers are standardized across all role families, ensuring that a senior designer is valued comparably to a senior engineer.

### Experience Multiplier

Within each role level, individuals gain experience and expertise that makes them more valuable. Our experience multiplier accounts for this growth.

For every year of relevant experience, team members receive a 3% increase in their experience multiplier, up to a maximum of 30% (10 years). This ensures that people are rewarded for continuously developing their expertise without having to constantly switch companies to get fair raises.

"Relevant experience" is defined broadly and collaboratively. We count professional work experience, significant open-source contributions, teaching, and other activities that genuinely develop expertise. What matters is growth in capability, not just time served.

### Performance Factor

The final component is our performance factor, which ranges from 0.9x to 1.2x and is assessed bi-annually.

We're honest about this being the most subjective part of our formula, but we work hard to make it as objective as possible. Performance is evaluated against clear goals set collaboratively at the beginning of each period. We use a 360-degree feedback process where peers, managers, and direct reports all contribute to performance assessments.

The performance factor serves two purposes: it rewards exceptional contributions, and it accounts for those rare situations where someone isn't meeting the expectations of their role level. Importantly, if someone consistently performs below 1.0x at their current level, it's usually a sign they need support, training, or perhaps that they're at the wrong role level.

## What We Eliminated: The Geographic Component

The most significant change in our new formula is what we removed: geographic-based pay differentiation.

This means someone doing the same work at the same level with the same experience receives the same salary whether they live in London, Lagos, or Louisville. This is our interpretation of "equal pay for equal work" in a remote-first world.

We know this is controversial. Many companies justify geographic pay differences by arguing that cost of living matters, that local market rates differ, or that paying "San Francisco salaries" globally would be unsustainable.

Here's our perspective: we're not paying "San Francisco salaries." We're paying fair remote-first market rates for excellent work. Geography doesn't determine value—contribution does.

Yes, this means some team members might earn more relative to their local cost of living than others. We're comfortable with that. We believe the alternative—paying people less because we can—is harder to justify ethically.

We also believe this approach is more sustainable long-term. As remote work becomes standard, the artificial geographic salary divisions will erode anyway. We're simply ahead of the curve.

## Our Updated Salary Calculator

Transparency means nothing if people can't actually see how it works in practice. That's why we've completely rebuilt our salary calculator and made it available on our website.

The new calculator allows anyone—whether you're a prospective team member, a current employee, or just a curious observer—to input role parameters and see exactly what that position would pay.

You can select:
- Role family (Engineering, Design, Marketing, etc.)
- Role level (Entry through Principal)
- Years of relevant experience
- Performance rating

The calculator instantly shows the resulting salary, along with a complete breakdown of how each component contributes to the total. You can also see the salary range for any given role (the difference between minimum and maximum performance factors) so you understand the full compensation band.

We've also added comparison features. You can see how different role levels compare within the same family, how the same role level compares across families, and how experience growth translates to compensation growth over time.

## Addressing Common Questions

As we've shared this new approach with our team and advisors, we've gotten several recurring questions that are worth addressing directly.

**"Won't this make you uncompetitive in high-cost markets?"**

Potentially, for some roles. A senior engineer in San Francisco might find comparable positions paying more at companies using traditional geographic multipliers. But we've found that many people value transparency, fairness, and flexibility more than maximizing absolute salary. We're okay attracting people who share those values.

**"Won't everyone just move to low-cost locations to maximize their purchasing power?"**

Some might, and that's fine. Where team members choose to live is their decision. We care about the work they do, not where they do it from. If living in a lower-cost area improves someone's quality of life while they continue doing excellent work, we consider that a win.

**"Isn't this just an excuse to pay people in developing countries less than market rate?"**

Actually, it's the opposite. Our formula often results in paying significantly above local market rates in many countries, which is intentional. We're hiring the best people globally and compensating them fairly, not engaging in wage arbitrage.

**"What about taxes and compliance costs that vary by location?"**

These are real costs, but they're our costs to manage, not reasons to pay individual contributors less. We budget for these variations at the company level rather than passing them through to individuals.

## What This Means for Our Customers

If you're reading this as a prospective or current customer, you might be wondering why you should care about our internal compensation philosophy.

Here's why it matters: how we treat our team directly affects how they serve you.

When people feel fairly compensated and trust that their colleagues are also fairly compensated, they bring their full energy to work. There's no resentment, no salary envy, no nagging feeling that they should be job-hunting. This stability means you work with team members who are fully present and invested in your success.

Our transparent approach to compensation reflects how we approach everything else. We believe in clear communication, fair dealing, and living our values even when it's difficult or expensive. These same principles guide how we work with customers.

Additionally, our remote-first compensation model means we can hire the absolute best people regardless of where they live. Your account team might include someone from Brazil, Bulgaria, and Boise—and you'll benefit from that diversity of perspective and experience.

We're especially focused on continuing to refine our role level rubrics to ensure they're truly objective and fair across different disciplines. We're also exploring how to better account for truly exceptional, transformative contributions that go beyond our standard performance factor range.

What won't change is our commitment to transparency. Every adjustment we make to our formula will be documented, explained, and reflected in real-time in our public calculator.

We believe the future of work is transparent, remote-first, and fundamentally more fair than the systems we're leaving behind. We're excited to be building that future, and we invite you to hold us accountable to these principles.
