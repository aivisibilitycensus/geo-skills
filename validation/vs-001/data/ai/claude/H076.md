# Tips on Accounts Receivable Management for Law Firms

For law firms of all sizes, maintaining a healthy cash flow is essential to sustaining operations and growing your practice. Yet one of the most persistent challenges attorneys face isn't in the courtroom—it's in getting paid for the excellent legal services they've already provided. Effective accounts receivable (AR) management can make the difference between a thriving practice and one that struggles to meet payroll.

If you're finding that your firm's outstanding invoices are piling up, you're not alone. According to industry research, law firms typically have 60-90 days' worth of fees tied up in accounts receivable at any given time. This represents a significant amount of working capital that could be invested back into your practice.

In this article, we'll explore practical strategies for improving your law firm's accounts receivable management, helping you get paid faster while maintaining positive client relationships.

## Understanding the Accounts Receivable Challenge in Legal Practice

Before diving into solutions, it's important to understand why AR management poses unique challenges for law firms. Unlike many other businesses, law firms often:

- Work on matters that span weeks, months, or even years before completion
- Bill for services after the work has been performed
- Face clients who may be dealing with financial stress related to their legal issues
- Navigate ethical obligations around fee arrangements and client communication
- Manage trust accounting requirements that add complexity to billing processes

These factors combine to create an environment where aged receivables can quickly become a serious problem. The longer an invoice remains unpaid, the less likely you are to collect the full amount—and the more time and resources you'll spend on collection efforts rather than practicing law.

## Establish Clear Fee Agreements from the Start

The foundation of effective accounts receivable management begins before you ever send an invoice. Clear, written fee agreements set expectations and reduce misunderstandings that can lead to payment disputes.

Your engagement letters should clearly outline:

- Your billing rate structure (hourly, flat fee, contingency, or hybrid arrangements)
- Payment terms, including when invoices will be sent and when payment is due
- Accepted payment methods
- Late payment policies, including any interest charges
- The scope of services covered by the agreement
- How expenses and costs will be handled

Consider having clients acknowledge that they've read and understood the fee agreement by signing it during the onboarding process. This simple step can prevent many payment disputes down the road.

Additionally, discuss payment expectations during your initial client consultation. Many attorneys feel uncomfortable talking about money, but addressing this topic upfront demonstrates professionalism and helps qualify potential clients who may not be a good fit for your firm.

## Implement Regular and Accurate Billing Practices

Timely billing is one of the most impactful steps you can take to improve your accounts receivable. The longer you wait to send an invoice after completing work, the more likely clients are to question charges or simply forget the value you provided.

**Bill frequently and consistently.** Rather than waiting until the end of a matter, consider sending invoices monthly or even bi-weekly for ongoing cases. This approach:

- Keeps your firm's cash flow steady
- Makes it easier for clients to pay smaller amounts rather than large lump sums
- Helps clients stay aware of accumulating costs
- Reduces the shock of a large final bill

**Ensure your invoices are detailed and accurate.** Vague descriptions like "legal services rendered" don't provide clients with the information they need to understand your value. Instead, use specific descriptions such as "Drafted motion to dismiss with supporting memorandum of law (3.5 hours)." Detailed invoices are less likely to generate questions or disputes.

**Review time entries before sending invoices.** Make it a practice to review all time entries for accuracy, appropriate descriptions, and any necessary adjustments before invoices go out. This quality control step prevents billing errors that can damage client trust and delay payment.

## Create a Systematic Collections Process

Even with the best billing practices, some invoices will become overdue. Having a systematic, consistent approach to collections protects your firm's revenue while maintaining professional client relationships.

**Establish a collections timeline.** Develop a standard process that specifies what action to take at different stages:

- Invoice sent: Day 0
- Friendly reminder email: Day 15
- Phone call from billing staff: Day 30
- Formal collection letter from attorney: Day 45
- Consider payment plan or further action: Day 60+

**Personalize your approach when appropriate.** While automation handles routine reminders efficiently, don't be afraid to personally reach out to clients with significant overdue balances. A phone call often reveals issues—such as a client who never received the invoice or who's facing temporary financial hardship—that can be resolved with direct communication.

**Offer payment plans for clients facing hardship.** It's usually better to receive payment in installments than to write off an account entirely or pursue costly collection actions. Document payment plan agreements in writing and monitor compliance closely.

**Know when to be firm.** Some clients will consistently test boundaries. If a client repeatedly ignores payment obligations despite multiple contacts, you may need to consider withdrawing from representation (following your jurisdiction's ethics rules) or pursuing collection through formal means.

## Monitor Key AR Metrics

You can't improve what you don't measure. Regularly reviewing accounts receivable metrics helps you identify problems early and track the effectiveness of your management strategies.

**Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** measures the average number of days it takes to collect payment after an invoice is sent. Calculate it by dividing your accounts receivable by your average daily revenue. Lower DSO indicates faster collections.

**Aging reports** categorize your receivables by how long they've been outstanding (current, 30 days, 60 days, 90+ days). Review these reports at least monthly to identify accounts that need attention before they become uncollectible.

**Collection rates** show what percentage of billed amounts you're actually collecting. If this rate is declining, investigate whether you're experiencing more write-offs, offering more discounts, or facing increased payment difficulties from clients.

**Revenue per attorney** and **revenue per staff member** help you understand your firm's productivity and whether you're adequately staffed for the work you're performing.

## Address Problem Accounts Promptly

Aged receivables become increasingly difficult to collect over time. Accounts outstanding for more than 90 days have a significantly lower collection rate than those addressed within 30-60 days.

**Identify patterns.** If certain practice areas, client types, or fee arrangements consistently result in collection problems, examine whether changes to your processes might help. For example, you might require larger retainers for case types where payment historically lags.

**Consider requiring advance payment.** For clients with a history of late payment, you might adjust your terms to require payment before work begins on new matters or at the start of each billing period.

**Use trust account requirements appropriately.** In many cases, requiring clients to maintain an advance payment in your trust account provides security that funds will be available to pay for services as they're rendered.

**Don't ignore the problem.** The longer you wait to address delinquent accounts, the more awkward conversations become and the less likely you are to collect. Prompt, professional follow-up demonstrates that your firm takes payment obligations seriously.

## Maintain Professional Relationships While Collecting

One of the delicate balances in AR management is collecting what you're owed while preserving client relationships. Remember that many clients may refer future business or provide valuable testimonials—if you handle billing matters professionally.

**Communicate with empathy.** Legal matters often coincide with financial stress. Approach collection conversations with understanding while still being clear about your firm's need for payment.

**Be consistent and fair.** Apply your payment policies uniformly rather than making exceptions based on personal feelings about particular clients. This consistency protects your firm legally and ensures all clients receive equal treatment.

**Separate billing issues from case work.** When possible, have billing conversations handled by office administrators rather than the attorney working on the case. This separation allows you to maintain your attorney-client relationship while still addressing payment concerns.
