# VS-001: Validating the Content Shape Checker

Pre-registered 29 October 2026, before any data was collected. Nothing below changes once collection starts, except to correct an error, and any correction is logged at the end of this file with its date.

## Question

How often does the Content Shape Checker, exactly as published, flag business articles written by people (false positives), and how often does it flag business articles generated by AI models (detections)?

## Tool under test

The checker's code as served at aivisibilitycensus.com on the day the study runs (version 1.1.0, the file `hsc.js`). Its SHA-256 hash is recorded with the results. No threshold or word list is changed during the study. Any change prompted by the results goes into a later version and is tested on a fresh sample.

## Human articles

- **Source:** 120 English business blog articles archived by the Wayback Machine on or before 31 October 2022, a month before ChatGPT's public launch on 30 November 2022.
- **Blogs:** 30 blogs in six groups of five, listed in Appendix A, fixed before sampling. Four articles per blog.
- **Draw:** for each blog, list the archived URLs captured between 1 January 2019 and 31 October 2022 that look like article pages (not tag, category, author, pagination or search pages). Shuffle them with a fixed random seed (20261029) and take candidates in that order.
- **Inclusion:** an article (not a landing page, product page, press release, job ad or glossary stub); English; 500 to 3,000 words of body text; at least three subheadings.
- **Replacement:** an excluded candidate is replaced by the next one in the shuffled order for the same blog. A blog with fewer than four eligible articles is replaced by the next blog in its group's reserve list (Appendix A), in order.
- **Snapshot:** the earliest capture of each article URL on or before 31 October 2022 is used.

## AI articles

- **Matching:** for each human article, each model writes one article with the same title, of similar length (the human article's word count, rounded to the nearest 100), with subheadings.
- **Prompt:** fixed, in Appendix B. One generation per title per model, default settings, no editing, no regeneration, no selection. Every generation is kept and reported.
- **Models:** Claude (Anthropic) first. GPT (OpenAI) and Gemini (Google) are added through their APIs before results are published. Each model is reported separately and in total.

## Text extraction

- **Human articles:** the archived HTML is reduced to the article body with Mozilla Readability (a standard, published method). Headings, paragraphs and list items are kept in order and converted to the same Markdown form the checker accepts. Navigation, sidebars, author boxes, calls to action and comments are removed by Readability, not by hand.
- **AI articles:** used as Markdown, as generated.
- **Both:** the article title (the top-level heading) is removed, because Readability removes it from human pages. Then both are passed through the checker's own `parseText` and `analyze` functions.

## Outcomes

**Primary.** An article is "flagged" when two or more of the four checks FAIL. Reported:

- the false-positive rate: the share of human articles flagged;
- the detection rate: the share of AI articles flagged;
- 95% Wilson intervals for both.

**Secondary:**

- the FAIL rate of each check, by corpus;
- the distribution of FAIL counts (0 to 4);
- how well the FAIL count separates the corpora (AUROC);
- results by blog group, length band and model;
- PASS WITH NOTES rates.

## Predictions, written before any data

These are guesses, recorded so the results can prove them wrong.

- Human articles flagged: between 15% and 35%.
- Claude articles flagged: between 55% and 85%.
- The check with the highest FAIL rate on human articles: scaffold variance, because business blogs use templates.
- The check that best separates the corpora: real things, named.

## Decision rules, fixed now

- If the upper 95% bound of the false-positive rate is above 35%, the tool page says so in plain words, and the tool stays positioned as editing notes, not evidence.
- If the detection rate is below 50%, the page says the checker does not reliably separate AI drafts from human ones.
- The results are published whatever they show, on the experiments page and with the data.

## Known limits, stated in advance

- **Not every pre-2022 article is a single person's own writing.** Some were ghostwritten, templated or heavily edited. They are still human-produced, which is the claim being tested.
- **The AI articles are unedited.** Real AI-assisted drafts are often edited, which this study does not measure.
- **The scope is narrow.** English business blogs only, and only version 1.1.0 of the tool.
- **The model mix is limited.** It is three model families, at most, at one point in time.

## What gets published

- this protocol, as written today;
- per-article results, with the URL, snapshot timestamp, word count and the outcome of each check;
- the full AI-generated texts, because we created them;
- the human texts are not republished; they are linked to their archived copies;
- the code, so anyone can rerun the study.

## Appendix B: AI prompt

> Write a blog article for a business website titled "{title}". Write about {words} words. Use subheadings. Write for the company's prospective customers.

Nothing else is added: no style instructions, no "sound human", no examples.
